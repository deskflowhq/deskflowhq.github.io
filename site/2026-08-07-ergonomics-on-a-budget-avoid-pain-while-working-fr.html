Ergonomics on a Budget: Avoid Pain While Working From Home

Published 2026-08-07

Working from home often means using whatever furniture you have. But poor posture can quickly lead to neck, back, and wrist pain. You don't need expensive equipment to improve your setup and feel better. This guide focuses on simple, cheap solutions for better ergonomics.

Why Ergonomics Matters

Bad posture over long periods causes strain, discomfort, and can lead to chronic pain or injury. Good ergonomics means setting up your workspace to support your body naturally, keeping you comfortable and productive without spending a fortune.

The Laptop Problem

Many new remote workers just place their laptop on a table. This forces you to look down, straining your neck and upper back.

**The Fix:** Raise your laptop screen to eye level.

**How:** Use a sturdy stack of books, a strong cardboard box, or a simple, cheap laptop stand (often found for under $20 online). The goal is for the top of your screen to be level with your eyes when you sit upright.

Your Chair: Make It Work

Most beginners use a dining chair or similar. While not ideal, you can make it better.

**Lumbar Support:** Your lower back needs support. Roll up a small towel, blanket, or even a thick sweatshirt. Place it horizontally behind the curve of your lower back. This helps maintain your spine's natural curve.

**Seat Depth:** Sit all the way back in your chair. There should be a small gap (a few fingers' width) between the back of your knees and the edge of the chair. If your chair is too deep, use a cushion or pillow behind you in addition to your lumbar support to push you forward.

**Armrests:** If your chair has armrests, try to keep your forearms parallel to the floor, with your elbows at about a 90-degree angle. If the armrests are too high or low and can't be adjusted, it's often better not to use them, or try to remove them if possible.

External Keyboard and Mouse (A Must-Have)

Once you raise your laptop screen, typing directly on it becomes uncomfortable. This is where an external keyboard and mouse become essential.

**Why:** They allow you to keep your hands and wrists in a neutral, comfortable position, separate from the screen.

**Cost:** Basic wired keyboards and mice can be bought as a set for under $20-30. Even wireless options are affordable. This is one of the most impactful cheap upgrades you can make.

**Placement:** Place your keyboard directly in front of you, close enough that your elbows are at about 90 degrees. Keep your mouse close by on the side you use it.

Foot Support (Don't Forget Your Feet!)

If your feet dangle or aren't flat on the floor, it can put extra pressure on your legs and lower back.

**The Fix:** Ensure your feet are flat on the floor.

**How:** If your chair is too high, use a sturdy box, a stack of old magazines or books, or a cheap step stool as a footrest. Your knees should be at about the same level as your hips, or slightly below, forming roughly a 90-degree angle.

Taking Breaks (Crucial!)

No matter how well you set up your space, prolonged sitting is not good.

**Rule of Thumb:** Get up and move around every 30-60 minutes.

**What to do:** Walk to get a glass of water, stretch, or just stand up for a minute or two. This improves circulation, reduces stiffness, and helps your mental focus.

Simple Stretches

During your breaks, do a few quick stretches to release tension.

* **Neck:** Gently tilt your head side to side, bringing your ear towards your shoulder. Roll your shoulders forward and backward.

* **Wrists:** Make gentle circles with your wrists.

* **Back:** Stand up and gently arch your back, then round it forward.

By implementing these simple, cheap changes, you can significantly improve your comfort and reduce the risk of pain while working from home. Your body will thank you!
