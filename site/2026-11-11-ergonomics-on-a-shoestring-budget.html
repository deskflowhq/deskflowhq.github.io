Ergonomics on a Shoestring Budget

Published 2026-11-11

Many people start remote work without a dedicated office setup. You might be using your kitchen table, a spare desk, or even the couch. This guide helps you improve your comfort and posture using what you already have or inexpensive items, without buying a new desk or fancy chair.

**The Problem**

Working directly on a laptop at a table often forces you to hunch over, straining your neck and back. Using it on your lap is even worse.

**Your Goal**

Create a more ergonomic setup to reduce strain, even with basic furniture and a very tight budget.

**Elevate Your Screen**

This is the most important step for improving your posture. Your eyes should be level with the top third of your screen.

* **Use books or boxes:** Stack old books, sturdy shoeboxes, or even a ream of printer paper under your laptop. Ensure it's stable and doesn't wobble.

* **DIY stand:** With some basic tools, you can even construct a simple stand from plywood scraps or sturdy cardboard.

* **Consider a laptop riser:** If you can spare $15-30, a simple plastic or metal laptop riser will do the trick and often folds up. This is a great first purchase if your budget allows.

**External Keyboard and Mouse**

Once your screen is elevated, your laptop keyboard will be too high. You need to separate your input devices to keep your hands and wrists comfortable.

* **Budget keyboard:** A basic wired USB keyboard costs as little as $10-15. Look for a full-size one for better comfort.

* **Budget mouse:** A simple wired USB mouse is just $5-10. Wireless options are slightly more but offer more freedom.

* **Positioning:** Place the keyboard and mouse directly in front of you. Your elbows should be at roughly a 90-degree angle, and your shoulders relaxed. Avoid reaching far forward.

**Improve Your Chair**

You might not have an expensive office chair, but you can make your existing kitchen or dining chair much better.

* **Lumbar support:** Roll up a towel or small blanket and place it horizontally at the small of your back. This supports your natural spine curve. You can also buy a basic lumbar pillow for $15-25.

* **Seat cushion:** If your chair is hard, a folded blanket or a simple seat cushion (often found for kitchen chairs) can add comfort.

* **Feet flat:** Your feet should be flat on the floor. If they dangle, use a shoebox, a stack of books, or a small stool as a footrest. This helps with lower back support and leg circulation.

**Lighting Matters**

Good lighting reduces eye strain and helps with concentration.

* **Natural light first:** Position yourself near a window if possible. Avoid glare on your screen by not sitting directly facing or with your back to the window.

* **Desk lamp:** If natural light is limited, use a basic desk lamp. Position it to illuminate your workspace, not to shine directly into your eyes or create glare on your screen. An inexpensive LED desk lamp is better than nothing.

**Take Frequent Breaks**

No setup is perfect without movement. Even the best ergonomic setup won't prevent discomfort if you sit for hours without moving.

* **The 20-20-20 rule:** Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds. This relaxes your eyes.

* **Stand and stretch:** Every hour, stand up, walk around, and gently stretch your body. Even a minute or two makes a big difference.

**Summary**

You don't need a fancy office to work comfortably. By elevating your screen, using separate input devices, adding support to your chair, minding your lighting, and taking frequent breaks, you can create a much healthier workspace with minimal investment. Start with what you have, then upgrade one small thing at a time if your budget allows.
