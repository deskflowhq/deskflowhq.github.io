Ergonomic Home Office for (Almost) Free

Published 2026-07-07

Welcome to setting up your first home office! You don't need fancy equipment to make your workspace more comfortable and reduce strain. This guide focuses on improving your ergonomics using items you likely already have, spending next to nothing.

Understanding Basic Ergonomics

Ergonomics is about designing your workspace to fit you, not the other way around. Good ergonomics prevents aches, pains, and fatigue, making you more productive. The goal is to maintain a neutral posture, meaning your joints are not bent awkwardly.

Screen Height: Your First Priority

Most people using a laptop on a table have their screen too low, leading to neck strain. Elevate your laptop or monitor so the top of the screen is roughly at eye level. Use a stack of sturdy books, thick shoeboxes, or even a ream of paper. If using a laptop, this means you'll need an external keyboard and mouse.

External Keyboard and Mouse (Crucial for Laptops)

If your laptop screen is elevated, you can't comfortably type on its keyboard. If you don't have them already, a basic USB keyboard and mouse are often the only purchases needed for this setup. You can find decent used ones for under $10 or new for around $20-30 total. Position them so your elbows are at about a 90-degree angle and your wrists are straight.

Improvised Wrist Support

If you find your wrists bending or aching, you can create a simple wrist rest. Fold a small towel or use a soft scarf. Place it just in front of your keyboard and mouse so your wrists stay straight and relaxed while typing and clicking, without putting pressure on the carpal tunnel area.

Comfortable Seating: Use What You Have

A dining chair isn't ideal for long hours, but you can improve it. For lower back support, roll up a towel or small blanket and place it behind the curve of your lower back. For extra height or cushioning, use a firm pillow or cushion on the seat. Your feet should be flat on the floor; if not, use a shoebox, stack of books, or a sturdy wastebasket as a makeshift footrest.

Taking Regular Breaks: Your Best Free Tool

Even a perfectly ergonomic setup can't beat sitting for hours. Set a timer for every 30-60 minutes. When it goes off, stand up, stretch, walk around for a few minutes, grab water, or look out a window. This is the single most effective ergonomic tool and it costs nothing.

Listen to Your Body

Pay attention to any discomfort. If your neck hurts, adjust your screen height. If your wrists ache, check your keyboard and mouse position and wrist support. Experiment with different arrangements until you find what feels best for you. Your body will tell you what needs tweaking.
