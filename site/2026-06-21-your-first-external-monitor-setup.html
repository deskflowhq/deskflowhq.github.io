Your First External Monitor Setup

Published 2026-06-21

Working from your laptop screen all day can lead to neck pain, eye strain, and lower productivity. This guide helps you set up a basic, ergonomic workstation using an external monitor, keyboard, and mouse without spending a lot. It assumes you already have a laptop.

Why You Need an External Monitor

An external monitor lets you look straight ahead, keeping your neck in a neutral position. It also gives you much more screen space, making multitasking easier and significantly boosting your productivity. You can use your laptop as a second screen or close it and just use the external monitor.

Choosing Your Monitor (Used is Best)

Don't buy new. The best budget option is a used monitor. Look for 22 to 24-inch models with 1080p resolution. Brands like Dell, HP, Lenovo, and Samsung often have durable, business-grade monitors available cheaply.

Where to look: Facebook Marketplace, Craigslist, local pawn shops, and thrift stores. Always check the screen for dead pixels, deep scratches, and make sure it powers on before you buy.

Look for common connection ports like HDMI or DisplayPort. HDMI is usually the easiest to connect.

Connecting Your Monitor

Most modern laptops have an HDMI port. If yours does, you just need an HDMI cable (often $5-10 new). If your laptop only has USB-C ports, you'll need a USB-C to HDMI adapter (around $15-25). Plug one end into your laptop and the other into the monitor. Then, go into your computer's display settings to extend or duplicate your desktop.

Keyboard and Mouse: Don't Skimp Too Much

While you can find used keyboards and mice, buying a basic new wired or wireless combo set for $15-30 is often worth it for hygiene and reliability. Look for simple sets from brands like Logitech or Microsoft. A separate keyboard and mouse are crucial for ergonomic positioning, allowing you to keep your wrists and arms in a comfortable, neutral position while typing and navigating.

Sound and Video Calls (Optional But Helpful)

Your laptop's built-in microphone and speakers are usually fine for basic calls. For better sound quality and privacy, a cheap pair of headphones with a built-in microphone (often called a headset) is a great upgrade. Basic models can be found for $15-30. If your laptop's webcam is poor, or you plan to keep your laptop closed, a basic used external webcam can be found for $10-25.

Making It Ergonomic (Budget Style)

Once everything is connected, set up your space:

1. **Monitor Height:** Place your external monitor directly in front of you. Raise it so the top third of the screen is at eye level. Use books, reams of paper, or a sturdy box if you don't have a monitor stand.

2. **Keyboard and Mouse Position:** Keep your keyboard and mouse close to your body, allowing your forearms to be parallel to the floor.

3. **Laptop Position:** If you're using your laptop as a second screen, place it to the side of your main monitor. If you're closing it, ensure it's on a breathable surface.

4. **Chair:** Use whatever chair you have, but try to sit with your feet flat on the floor and your back supported. Take regular breaks to stand and stretch.

Total Estimated Cost

* Used 22-24 inch Monitor: $40 - $80

* HDMI Cable: $5 - $10

* USB-C to HDMI Adapter (if needed): $15 - $25

* New Basic Keyboard and Mouse Combo: $15 - $30

* Headphones with Mic (optional): $15 - $30

* Used Webcam (optional): $10 - $25

Total estimated cost for a functional, more ergonomic setup: **$75 - $180**. This investment will significantly improve your comfort and productivity for remote work.
