Basic Home Office Under $100

Published 2026-07-24

Your Goal: A Usable Workspace

Starting remote work or a home office doesn't have to be expensive. This guide focuses on getting a functional, dedicated workspace for under $100. The goal isn't fancy, but effective and comfortable enough for daily use.

The "Must Haves"

To work efficiently, you need four core things:

1. A dedicated surface (desk).

2. A supportive place to sit (chair).

3. Your computer (laptop or desktop).

4. Basic tools to control your computer (keyboard and mouse).

Desk: Get Creative (Budget: $0-$50)

The desk is your primary workspace. You don't need a fancy office desk right away.

* **Use What You Have:** A dining table, kitchen table, or even a sturdy card table can serve as an excellent temporary desk. This costs $0.

* **Folding Table:** If you need something dedicated, a new 4-foot or 5-foot folding table (like those used for parties) is often available at stores like Walmart or Target for $30-$50. These are lightweight, easy to set up, and sturdy enough.

* **Avoid the Couch or Bed:** While tempting, working from your couch or bed is bad for your posture and productivity long-term. Get a proper surface.

Chair: Comfort Over Style (Budget: $0-$50)

Your chair is crucial for preventing back pain and fatigue.

* **Kitchen or Dining Chair:** Start with the sturdiest, most comfortable chair you already own. Add a firm pillow for lumbar support (the curve of your lower back) if needed. This costs $0.

* **Basic Task Chair:** If you need to buy one, look for a new basic task chair from stores like IKEA, Walmart, or Target for $30-$50. Focus on one that lets your feet rest flat on the floor (or use a box as a footrest) and supports your back.

* **Used Options:** Check local thrift stores, Facebook Marketplace, or Craigslist for used office chairs. You can often find decent ones for $20-$40, but inspect them for stability and cleanliness.

Your Computer: Use What You Already Have (Budget: $0)

This guide assumes you already have a laptop or a desktop computer.

* **Laptop Ergonomics:** If using a laptop, it's best to elevate it so the top of the screen is at eye level. You can use a stack of sturdy books, a shoebox, or even a basic laptop stand (which can be found for $15-$25 if your budget allows). If you elevate your laptop, you'll need an external keyboard and mouse.

Basic Peripherals: Keyboard, Mouse (Budget: $15-$30)

If you're using a laptop elevated on a stand (which is highly recommended), or if you have a desktop, you'll need these.

* **Wired Set:** A basic wired keyboard and mouse combo can be bought new for $15-$30. Stores like Amazon, Best Buy, or even general stores like Target carry many options. They plug directly into your computer's USB port and work instantly.

* **Consider Comfort:** Even on a budget, choose a keyboard and mouse that feel reasonably comfortable in your hands.

Lighting: Don't Strain Your Eyes (Budget: $0-$15)

Good lighting reduces eye strain and helps you stay alert.

* **Natural Light:** Position your desk near a window if possible, but avoid direct glare on your screen.

* **Existing Lamps:** Use an existing lamp you have in the house.

* **Basic Desk Lamp:** If you need one, a simple, flexible desk lamp with an LED bulb can be purchased for $10-$15. Position it to illuminate your workspace without shining directly into your eyes or reflecting off your screen.

Budget Breakdown Example ($100 Total)

* Folding Table: $40

* Basic Task Chair: $40

* Wired Keyboard & Mouse Combo: $20

* **Total: $100**

Pro Tip: Start Small, Upgrade Later

Your first home office doesn't need to be perfect. Get the absolute basics to establish a dedicated, comfortable-enough workspace. As you use it, you'll learn what specific upgrades would truly benefit you, and you can save up for those one by one.
