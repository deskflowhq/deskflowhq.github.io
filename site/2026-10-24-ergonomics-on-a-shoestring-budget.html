Ergonomics on a Shoestring Budget

Published 2026-10-24

Many people start remote work without a dedicated home office. This guide helps you make your existing kitchen table, dining table, or spare desk work for your body, even if you can't buy new furniture. Your health is important!

**1. Adjust Your Chair (or Seat)**

* **What you have:** A kitchen chair, dining chair, or stool.

* **Goal:** Your feet should be flat on the floor, hips slightly above your knees, and your back supported.

* **How:** If your chair is too low, use firm cushions (like couch cushions or firm pillows) to raise your sitting height. If it's too high and your feet dangle, find a footrest. A stack of old books, a sturdy cardboard box, or even an upside-down laundry basket can work.

* **Back support:** Roll up a small towel or use a throw pillow for lumbar support. Place it at the curve of your lower back, just above your waistline.

**2. Optimize Your Screen Height**

* **What you have:** A laptop or an external monitor on a table.

* **Goal:** The top of your screen should be roughly at eye level. This prevents you from craning your neck down.

* **How:** If using a laptop, place it on a stack of sturdy books, reams of printer paper, or a stable box. This raises the screen to the correct height. For this to work comfortably, you will need an external keyboard and mouse (see the next point).

* If using an external monitor, do the same: elevate it with books or a stable box until the top of the screen is at eye level.

**3. Get an External Keyboard and Mouse**

* **Why it's cheap:** You might already have an old set in a drawer, or you can find a basic wired keyboard and mouse set for under $20-$30 online or at a local store.

* **Why it's important:** Once you raise your laptop screen, you can no longer comfortably type on its built-in keyboard. An external keyboard and mouse allow your wrists to stay in a neutral, flat position, and your shoulders to relax.

* **Placement:** Keep the keyboard close enough so your elbows are at about a 90-degree angle, close to your body. The mouse should be right beside it, easy to reach without stretching your arm.

**4. Improve Your Lighting**

* **Goal:** Good, even lighting helps reduce eye strain and makes you look better on video calls. Avoid harsh overhead lights or bright windows directly behind you.

* **How:** Position your workspace so natural light (from a window) is *in front* of you or *to your side*, not behind you. If working at night, use a desk lamp (even a bedside lamp can work) to illuminate your work area. Avoid working in a dark room with only the screen light.

**5. Take Regular Breaks**

* **This costs nothing:** Even the best setup isn't perfect if you sit for hours without moving.

* **How:** Every 30-60 minutes, stand up, stretch, and walk around for a few minutes. Get some water. Also, practice the "20-20-20 rule" for your eyes: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for at least 20 seconds. This helps reduce eye fatigue.

You don't need expensive equipment to start protecting your body while working remotely. Use what you have, make these small adjustments, and remember to move often. Your future self will thank you.
