Free Ergonomics at Home

Published 2026-07-09

If you've suddenly found yourself working from your kitchen table or a spare corner, you know the struggle. Back pain, neck strain, and general discomfort are common when using furniture not designed for all-day work. The good news is you can make huge improvements to your posture and comfort using things you already own, often for free. This guide helps you set up a basic ergonomic workstation with zero or minimal cost.

**Start with Your Chair**

Your chair is the foundation. Use a sturdy dining chair or kitchen chair.

* **Pull it close:** Sit as close to your table or desk as possible.

* **Sit tall:** Aim for your lower back to be supported by the chair back.

* **DIY lumbar support:** If your chair has a gap or doesn't support your lower back, roll up a small towel or use a throw pillow. Place it in the curve of your lower back.

**Raise Your Screen to Eye Level**

This is often the biggest game-changer. Most people look down at their laptop screens, leading to neck and shoulder pain.

* **Goal:** The top of your screen should be roughly at eye level. Your eyes should naturally look slightly down at the middle of the screen.

* **Instant riser:** Stack sturdy books, reams of printer paper, or even robust shoe boxes under your laptop or monitor until it reaches the correct height. Ensure it's stable and won't wobble.

* **Important:** If you raise your laptop, you will need an external keyboard and mouse.

**External Keyboard and Mouse (If You Raised Your Laptop)**

Once your laptop screen is at eye level, its built-in keyboard will be too high to use comfortably.

* **The only potential cost:** A basic wired keyboard and mouse set can cost as little as $15-30 if you need to buy them new.

* **Free options:** Ask friends or family if they have old USB keyboards or mice lying around. Many people do! Check local "buy nothing" groups online.

* **Placement:** Place the keyboard and mouse directly in front of you on the table, so your arms are close to your body.

**Proper Arm and Wrist Position**

Once your screen and input devices are set, fine-tune your arm position.

* **Elbows at 90 degrees:** Your elbows should be bent at roughly a 90-degree angle, close to your body, when you type and use the mouse.

* **Straight wrists:** Keep your wrists straight, not bent up, down, or to the side. Avoid resting your wrists on the table while typing.

**Foot Support for Better Posture**

Your feet need to be flat on the floor to maintain good lower back posture and circulation.

* **If your feet dangle:** If your chair is too high or you're using a counter, find something to rest your feet on. A sturdy box, old phone books, or a stack of heavy magazines can work as a footrest.

**Take Frequent Breaks**

Even with the perfect setup, sitting still for too long isn't healthy.

* **The 20-20-20 rule:** Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for at least 20 seconds. This helps reduce eye strain.

* **Stand and stretch:** Every 30-60 minutes, stand up, stretch, and walk around for a few minutes. Grab a glass of water, step away from the screen.

**Quick Ergonomic Checklist**

Before you settle in for your workday, do a quick check:

* Is the top of your screen at eye level?

* Are your feet flat on the floor or a stable footrest?

* Are your elbows bent at roughly 90 degrees when you type?

* Is your lower back supported?

* Are your wrists straight, not bent?

Making these simple adjustments can dramatically improve your comfort and productivity without breaking the bank.
