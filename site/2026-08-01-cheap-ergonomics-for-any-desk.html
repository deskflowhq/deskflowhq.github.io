Cheap Ergonomics for Any Desk

Published 2026-08-01

Working from home shouldn't cause you pain. You don't need expensive equipment to make your workspace more comfortable and healthy. This guide focuses on simple, budget-friendly ways to improve ergonomics using what you might already have.

Why Bother with Ergonomics?

Ergonomics is about designing your workspace to fit you, not the other way around. Good ergonomics prevents aches, pains, and long-term injuries. It helps you focus better and feel less tired at the end of the day.

Your Chair: Comfort First

You don't need a fancy ergonomic chair. Use a stable dining chair or whatever you have.

* **Support your lower back:** Roll up a towel or use a small throw pillow and place it behind the curve of your lower back.

* **Feet flat:** Your feet should be flat on the floor. If they dangle, stack some books or use a sturdy shoebox as a footrest. This helps your posture.

* **Sit back:** Lean back into your chair so your back is supported. Avoid perching on the edge.

Get Your Screen Eye-Level

The top of your screen should be roughly at eye level. This prevents neck strain from looking down or craning up.

* **Laptop users:** You absolutely need to raise your laptop. Stack sturdy books, a strong cardboard box, or a ream of paper beneath it until the top of the screen is at eye level.

* **Monitor users:** If your monitor is too low, use the same trick: stack books or a box underneath its stand.

* **Distance:** Position your screen about an arm's length away from your face.

Keyboard & Mouse: Relax Your Wrists

If you raise your laptop screen, you'll need an external keyboard and mouse. Even the cheapest ones are better for your posture than typing directly on a raised laptop.

* **Flat wrists:** Keep your wrists straight and flat when typing and using the mouse. Avoid bending them up or down.

* **Close to you:** Keep your keyboard and mouse close to your body to avoid reaching too far. Your elbows should be bent at about a 90-degree angle.

* **Pillow assist:** If your desk is too high and you can't adjust it, a small, flat pillow or folded towel under your forearms can help support them.

Light, Movement, and Breaks

Ergonomics isn't just about how you sit; it's also about your environment and habits.

* **Good lighting:** Position your desk near a window for natural light. If that's not possible, use a desk lamp to brighten your workspace and reduce eye strain. Avoid glare on your screen.

* **Take breaks:** The best ergonomic advice is to move! Stand up, stretch, and walk around for 5-10 minutes every hour. Set a timer as a reminder.

* **Listen to your body:** If something feels uncomfortable, adjust it. Small changes can make a big difference over time.

Your Budget Ergonomics Checklist

* Lower back supported (towel/pillow).

* Feet flat on the floor or on a footrest (books/box).

* Top of screen at eye level (books/box under laptop/monitor).

* External keyboard and mouse (if using a raised laptop).

* Wrists flat and straight.

* Take regular breaks to move around.
