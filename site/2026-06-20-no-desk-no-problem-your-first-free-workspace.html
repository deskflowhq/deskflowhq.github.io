No Desk, No Problem: Your First Free Workspace

Published 2026-06-20

You’ve started remote work or a side hustle, but you don't have a dedicated desk and can't afford one yet. Don't worry! You can create a functional, free workspace using things you already own. This guide helps you set up a basic, ergonomic-ish spot without spending a dime.

**Use What You Have**

Look around your home. Do you have a dining table, kitchen counter, coffee table, or even a sturdy chest of drawers? These can all serve as your temporary desk. The goal is a flat, stable surface where you can place your laptop or work materials.

**Choose Your Spot Wisely**

Pick a location that offers the fewest distractions. A corner of your dining room might be better than the middle of a high-traffic living room. Try to face a wall or window rather than the TV or a busy hallway. Good lighting is also important, preferably natural light from a window.

**Elevate Your Screen**

This is crucial for good posture. If you're using a laptop, your screen should be roughly eye-level. Use books, sturdy boxes, or even a stack of old magazines to raise your laptop. If you use an external keyboard and mouse (highly recommended for ergonomics, even cheap ones), you can place your laptop on the riser and type below. If not, just raise the laptop as much as possible and try to keep your wrists flat.

**Comfort is Key**

Use a dining chair or any firm chair you have. If it’s too hard, grab a pillow or folded blanket for cushioning. If your feet don't reach the floor comfortably (they should be flat, or nearly flat), use a shoebox, stack of books, or a sturdy footrest as a foot prop. Good posture prevents aches and pains. Sit up straight, shoulders relaxed, and arms at roughly a 90-degree angle when typing.

**Clear the Clutter**

Before you start, clear your chosen "desk" surface. A clean space helps you focus. Keep only what you absolutely need for work within arm's reach. After your workday, pack everything away to reclaim your space and mentally switch off.

**Make it Temporary**

Remember, this is a temporary solution. The advantage is it costs nothing and can be set up and packed away daily. This helps you define work time and personal time, especially important when working from shared spaces.

**Future Upgrades (Budget-Friendly)**

Once you're earning, consider small, impactful upgrades. A cheap external keyboard and mouse (often found for under $20 used or new) make a huge difference for ergonomics. A simple lamp can improve lighting. But for now, focus on making your free setup work effectively.
