Cheap Ergonomics for Your Home Office

Published 2026-10-07

Starting a home office doesn't mean sacrificing comfort or your body. You can make significant ergonomic improvements without spending much money, often using things you already own. This guide focuses on simple, practical adjustments to make your existing workspace healthier and more comfortable.

**Your Chair: Make it Work for You**

Don't have a fancy office chair? No problem. Your goal is to support your back and make sure your feet are comfortable.

* **Back Support:** Use a small pillow, a rolled-up towel, or a folded blanket in the curve of your lower back. This helps maintain your spine's natural curve.

* **Seat Comfort:** If your chair is too hard, add a cushion, a folded blanket, or even an extra pillow to sit on.

* **Foot Support:** Your feet should be flat on the floor. If they dangle, use a sturdy box, a stack of books, or a small step stool as a footrest. Avoid letting your feet hang unsupported.

**Screen Height: Eye Level is Key**

Looking up or down at your screen for hours can cause neck and shoulder strain.

* **Elevate Your Screen:** Place your monitor or laptop on a stack of sturdy books, a strong cardboard box, or reams of paper.

* **Target Height:** The top of your screen should be roughly level with your eyes when you are sitting up straight. This keeps your head in a neutral, comfortable position.

**Keyboard and Mouse: Close and Comfortable**

Your arms and shoulders will thank you for keeping your input devices in the right spot.

* **Keep Them Close:** Position your keyboard directly in front of you, centered with your body. Your mouse should be right beside it.

* **Elbow Angle:** Your elbows should be at about a 90-degree angle (or slightly wider) when your hands are on the keyboard. Avoid reaching far forward, as this strains your shoulders.

**Laptop Users: Elevate and Separate**

If a laptop is your primary work tool, this is the most important ergonomic adjustment you can make.

* **Elevate the Laptop Screen:** Just like a monitor, raise your laptop using books or a box until the top of the screen is at eye level (see "Screen Height" above).

* **Use External Peripherals:** Because your laptop is now elevated, you can't comfortably type on its keyboard. Invest in a basic, wired USB keyboard and mouse. You can find these for $20-$30 at most electronics stores or online. This allows you to keep your hands and wrists in a comfortable position while your screen is at the correct height. This one purchase makes a huge difference.

**Take Frequent Breaks: The Free Fix**

Even the most perfectly ergonomic setup won't save you from sitting for too long. This is the simplest, most effective "fix" and it costs nothing.

* **Stand Up and Move:** Set a timer for 25-30 minutes. When it goes off, stand up, stretch, walk around for 1-2 minutes, or grab a drink of water.

* **Eye Breaks (20-20-20 Rule):** Every 20 minutes, look away from your screen at something 20 feet away for at least 20 seconds. This helps reduce eye strain.

Implementing these small, mostly free adjustments can drastically improve your comfort and health when working from home, even on the tightest budget.
