Your First $100 Home Office

Published 2026-06-19

Starting remote work or setting up a home office doesn't need to break the bank. This guide focuses on getting a functional, comfortable setup for around $100 (excluding your computer itself). The goal is practical basics so you can get to work efficiently.

**The Foundation: Your Desk**

You need a dedicated surface. Don't immediately buy new.

* **Look around your home first.** Can a kitchen table, dining table, or even a sturdy console table serve as a temporary or permanent desk?

* **Consider folding tables.** A small folding utility table can be very affordable, easy to set up, and put away if space is tight.

* **Shop second-hand.** Check local thrift stores, Facebook Marketplace, or Craigslist for simple student desks or small tables. You can often find something sturdy for $20-$40.

**Comfort is Key: Your Chair**

A good chair prevents back pain. While ergonomic chairs are expensive, you can start simple.

* **Use a dining chair.** A firm dining chair with a straight back is often better than a soft, slouchy armchair.

* **Add a cushion.** If your chair is hard, a simple seat cushion or a small pillow for back support can make a big difference for comfort. These are inexpensive.

* **Consider a basic office chair.** If you find a very simple, no-frills office chair on sale or second-hand for $30-$50, it could be a good investment. Prioritize adjustability for height if possible.

**Essential Tech Accessories**

You likely have a laptop or computer. These items improve usability and ergonomics.

* **External Mouse:** A simple wired USB mouse is reliable and affordable. It's much more comfortable and precise than a laptop trackpad for extended work. Expect to pay $10-$15.

* **External Keyboard:** If you're on a laptop, using an external keyboard lets you position your screen at a better height, reducing neck strain. A basic wired USB keyboard costs around $15-$20.

* **Headphones with Microphone:** Crucial for calls and online meetings. Basic wired earbuds or a simple over-ear headset with a built-in microphone are widely available for $15-$25.

**Lighting Your Workspace**

Good lighting reduces eye strain and helps focus.

* **Maximize natural light.** Position your desk near a window if possible. Avoid having the window directly behind you (causes screen glare) or directly in front (causes eye strain).

* **Use a simple lamp.** Repurpose a lamp from another room. If buying, a basic desk lamp or even a clip-on LED light is sufficient. Look for LED bulbs for energy efficiency.

**Staying Organized**

A tidy space helps maintain focus.

* **Declutter regularly.** Only keep what you actively need on your desk.

* **Use simple containers.** A shoebox, a small plastic bin, or even a repurposed mug can hold pens, notepads, and small items. You don't need fancy organizers.

**Putting It All Together (Sample Budget)**

Here’s how these items might fit into your $100 goal:

* **Desk:** Used small desk from Facebook Marketplace - $30

* **Chair:** Existing dining chair + new seat cushion - $10

* **Mouse:** Basic wired USB mouse - $10

* **Keyboard:** Basic wired USB keyboard - $15

* **Headset:** Simple wired earbuds with mic - $20

* **Lamp:** Basic clip-on LED lamp - $10

* **Organization:** Repurposed mug and shoebox - $0

* **Total: $95**

This example shows how easy it is to set up a functional, comfortable, and cheap home office without spending a fortune. Start simple, focus on your needs, and upgrade later if necessary.
