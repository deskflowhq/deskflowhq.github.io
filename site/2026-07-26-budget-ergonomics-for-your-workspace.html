Budget Ergonomics for Your Workspace

Published 2026-07-26

Working from home shouldn't mean constant aches and pains. You don't need expensive gear to be comfortable. This guide shows you how to improve your workspace ergonomics using things you already own or very cheap items. The goal is to reduce strain and prevent common issues like neck, back, and wrist pain.

Why Ergonomics Matters

Good ergonomics helps you work longer and more comfortably. It prevents long-term injuries and boosts your focus. Even small adjustments can make a big difference to your body.

Your Chair: The Foundation

Start with your chair. Any chair can be improved.

* **Back Support:** Roll up a towel or use a small cushion. Place it against the curve of your lower back. This supports your lumbar spine.

* **Seat Height:** Your feet should be flat on the floor, and your knees at about a 90-degree angle. If your chair is too low, add a firm cushion to sit on. If it's too high and can't adjust, see the "Foot Support" section.

* **Armrests:** If your chair has armrests, adjust them so your shoulders are relaxed and your elbows are close to your body. If not, try to keep your forearms supported by the desk, but avoid shrugging your shoulders.

Screen Height: Eye Level

This is crucial for neck comfort.

* **Raise Your Screen:** Use a stack of sturdy books, old shoeboxes, or even reams of paper to raise your laptop or monitor.

* **Position It Right:** The top third of your screen should be at eye level. This means you look slightly down to view the rest of the screen. Your screen should be about an arm's length away.

Keyboard and Mouse: Neutral Wrists

Keeping your wrists straight is key to avoiding wrist and hand pain.

* **External Setup:** If you raised your laptop, you absolutely need an external keyboard and mouse. You can find basic sets for under $20. Plug them into your laptop.

* **Positioning:** Place your keyboard directly in front of you. Keep your mouse close by. Your elbows should be at your sides, bent at a 90-100 degree angle. Your wrists should be flat, not bent up or down.

Foot Support: Grounded Comfort

Your feet should always be flat on the floor or a footrest.

* **Make a Footrest:** If your feet don't touch the floor comfortably when your chair is at the right height, use a shoebox, a stack of thick books, or a sturdy plastic container as a footrest.

* **Leg Position:** Keep your knees slightly apart and your legs at a comfortable 90-degree angle or slightly more open.

Breaks: Your Best Ergonomic Tool

No perfect setup can replace movement.

* **Stand Up Often:** Every 30-60 minutes, stand up, stretch, and walk around for a minute or two.

* **Eye Breaks:** Look away from your screen every 20 minutes for 20 seconds. Focus on something far away (like outside a window).

Small Changes, Big Difference

You don't need to buy expensive gear. By using household items and being mindful of your posture, you can create a much more comfortable and healthier home office setup without spending a lot. Listen to your body and adjust as needed.
