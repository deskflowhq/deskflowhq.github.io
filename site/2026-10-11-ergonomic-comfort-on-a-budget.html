Ergonomic Comfort on a Budget

Published 2026-10-11

Working from home doesn't have to mean back pain or neck strain. Good ergonomics helps you stay comfortable, focused, and healthy, even if you can't buy expensive gear. This guide shows you how to improve your setup using what you might already have or cheap additions.

**Your Chair is Key**

You don't need a fancy ergonomic chair right away. Start with the chair you have.

* **Support your back:** Use a small pillow, a rolled-up towel, or a firm cushion behind your lower back. This helps maintain the natural curve of your spine.

* **Feet flat on the floor:** Your feet should be flat on the floor or a stable footrest. If your chair is too high, use a stack of sturdy books, a shoebox, or even a yoga block as a footrest. Avoid dangling feet.

* **Armrests (if you have them):** Adjust them so your shoulders are relaxed and your arms form roughly a 90-degree angle at the elbow when typing. If you don't have them, try to keep your forearms supported on the desk without shrugging your shoulders.

**Monitor Height Matters**

Looking down all day strains your neck. Your monitor should be at eye level.

* **The top of your screen:** The very top of your monitor screen should be roughly level with your eyes. This allows you to look slightly downward without craning your neck.

* **DIY stand:** Stack sturdy books, reams of paper, or a sturdy box underneath your monitor or laptop. Make sure it's stable and won't wobble.

* **Laptop users:** If you work primarily on a laptop, consider getting an external keyboard and mouse. Then, you can raise your laptop to eye level using a stand or stack of books.

**Keyboard and Mouse Position**

Keep your wrists straight and relaxed.

* **Close to your body:** Position your keyboard and mouse close to your body, so you don't have to reach far. This prevents shoulder and arm strain.

* **Flat wrists:** When typing, your wrists should be flat, not bent up or down. Your forearms should be roughly parallel to the floor.

* **Pillow or towel wrist rest:** If you feel strain, a small, soft towel or an old sock filled with rice can serve as a makeshift wrist rest for your keyboard or mouse. Avoid leaning on it too much; it's meant as a gentle guide.

**Take Breaks**

This is the cheapest and most effective ergonomic tool.

* **Stand up and move:** Aim to stand up and walk around for 5-10 minutes every hour. Stretch your arms, roll your shoulders, and walk to get water.

* **Look away:** Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds. This "20-20-20 rule" helps reduce eye strain.

**Lighting and Glare**

Good lighting reduces eye fatigue.

* **Avoid direct glare:** Position your monitor so there's no direct light from a window or bright lamp reflecting off the screen.

* **Even lighting:** Try to have general room lighting that's not too dim or too bright. A cheap desk lamp from a dollar store or an old lamp can add focused light to your workspace if needed.

* **Natural light:** If possible, position your desk near a window (parallel to it, not facing or with your back to it) to enjoy natural light without glare.
