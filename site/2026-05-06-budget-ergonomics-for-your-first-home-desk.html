Budget Ergonomics for Your First Home Desk

Published 2026-05-06

You've started working from home, and your existing table and chair are doing the job. But after a few hours, your neck or back starts to ache. You don't need to buy expensive furniture to feel better. This guide focuses on making your current setup more comfortable for free or very cheap.

**Start with What You Have**

Before buying anything, look at your current table or desk and the chair you use. We'll make small adjustments to these first. Don't worry about buying a new "ergonomic" chair or desk right away.

**Screen Height is Key**

The biggest culprit for neck pain is often a low screen.

* **Problem:** If you're using a laptop, the screen is usually too far below your eye level, making you crane your neck down.

* **Solution:** Raise your laptop! Stack a sturdy pile of books, a shoebox, a ream of paper, or even spare planks of wood underneath it.

* **Goal:** The top of your screen should be roughly at eye level when you sit up straight. This helps keep your neck in a neutral, comfortable position.

**Keyboard and Mouse Comfort**

Once your laptop is raised, typing on its keyboard won't be comfortable.

* **Problem:** Your wrists will be bent at an awkward angle if you type on a raised laptop.

* **Solution:** Invest in a separate, inexpensive external keyboard and mouse. Wired versions are very affordable (often under $15 each at big box stores or online).

* **Placement:** Place the external keyboard and mouse directly in front of you. Your forearms should be roughly parallel to the floor, and your elbows bent at about a 90-100 degree angle when you type. This keeps your wrists straight.

**Chair Support**

Even a basic chair can be improved with simple tweaks.

* **Problem:** Many chairs lack good lower back (lumbar) support, or they're too high/low.

* **Solution 1 (Lower Back):** Roll up a small hand towel, a t-shirt, or use a small throw pillow. Place it horizontally at the curve of your lower back. Adjust until it feels supportive and encourages a natural upright posture.

* **Solution 2 (Seat Height/Feet):** Your feet should be flat on the floor or a footrest. If your chair is too high and your feet dangle, find a sturdy box, a stack of books, or even an old phone book to use as a footrest. This takes pressure off your legs and lower back.

**Lighting Matters**

Good lighting reduces eye strain and can boost your mood.

* **Problem:** Working in a dimly lit room or with bright glare on your screen can strain your eyes.

* **Solution:** Position your desk near a window if possible, allowing natural light to come from the side, not directly in front or behind you (which can cause glare). If you don't have a window, use an existing desk lamp or floor lamp to provide soft, even lighting for your workspace. Avoid harsh overhead lights that cast shadows.

**Take Regular Breaks**

No matter how perfectly ergonomic your setup is, sitting for too long is bad for you.

* **Solution:** Set a timer for every 45-60 minutes. When it goes off, stand up, stretch, walk around your home, grab a drink of water, or look out a window for 5-10 minutes. These mini-breaks are crucial for your physical and mental well-being.
