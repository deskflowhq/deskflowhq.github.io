Improvise Your First Desk

Published 2026-06-01

Starting remote work often means you don't have a dedicated desk. This guide shows you how to create a functional, free, or nearly free workspace using things you already have at home. The goal is to get you set up comfortably without buying any new furniture.

Find Your Spot

Look for a quiet area with good natural light. A dining table, kitchen counter, or a seldom-used corner of a bedroom are great places to start. Consider areas where you won't be easily distracted by family or roommates.

The Surface Solution

Your "desk" can be anything sturdy and flat.

A dining table is excellent; just remember to clear it daily.

A kitchen counter offers a standing desk option for part of the day.

An unused dresser or bookshelf can also work if it's the right height.

For a truly temporary option, stack two sturdy storage bins or boxes and place a flat piece of cardboard or plywood on top.

Sitting Smartly

Use your most comfortable dining chair. Add a firm pillow or folded blanket to the seat for extra height or comfort. A small pillow behind your lower back can provide support and improve posture. Avoid working from a couch or bed for long periods, as this can lead to discomfort.

Elevate Your Screen

If you're using a laptop, stack some sturdy books or a small box underneath it. The goal is to raise the top of your screen to eye level. This prevents neck strain. You will likely need an external mouse and keyboard for comfort, which can be found very cheaply if you don't have them.

Power and Cables

Make sure your spot is near an electrical outlet. If not, use an extension cord to reach your device chargers. Use twist ties, rubber bands, or even masking tape to bundle cables together. This keeps your space tidy and prevents tripping hazards.

Personalize and Protect

Place a placemat, a piece of cardboard, or a large cutting board on your surface. This protects the original furniture from scratches and marks. Add one small personal item, like a potted plant or a photo, to make the space feel more like "yours."

Daily Pack-Up

Since your space might be temporary, make it easy to set up and take down. At the end of your workday, clear everything away. Designate a "work box" or a specific shelf where you store your laptop, charger, mouse, and any notebooks. This helps you mentally separate work from home life and keeps your shared spaces functional for everyone.
