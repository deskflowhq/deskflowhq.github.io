Simple Ergonomics for Any Budget

Published 2026-09-18

Working from home often means using the furniture you already own. You don't need expensive gear to make your workspace comfortable and prevent aches. Good ergonomics are about positioning your body correctly, and you can achieve a lot with simple, cheap tricks.

**Your Chair: Build a Better Seat**

You likely have a kitchen or dining chair. Start with that.

* **Seat Cushion**: Add a firm pillow or a folded blanket to the seat. This can raise your height and add comfort. Aim for your hips to be slightly higher than your knees.

* **Back Support**: Roll up a towel or use a small throw pillow. Place it in the curve of your lower back (the lumbar region). This supports your natural spine curve and prevents slouching.

* **Feet Flat**: Your feet should be flat on the floor. If they dangle, use a sturdy box, a stack of books, or a small step stool as a simple footrest.

**Your Desk: Get the Right Height**

Any stable table can be your desk. The goal is a comfortable height for your arms.

* **Elbows at 90 Degrees**: When you sit, your forearms should be roughly parallel to the floor, and your elbows should form about a 90-degree angle when you type.

* **Laptop Stand Alternative**: If you're using a laptop, stack sturdy books, a strong shoebox, or unused reams of paper to raise it. The top of your screen should be at eye level.

* **External Keyboard and Mouse**: This is often the cheapest and most impactful upgrade for laptop users. A basic USB keyboard and mouse cost very little (often $15-30 total). This lets you raise your laptop screen for better posture without forcing your arms and wrists into awkward positions. Position them so your wrists are straight.

**Screen Placement: Protect Your Eyes and Neck**

Once your screen is raised, refine its position.

* **Eye Level**: The top edge of your screen should be level with your eyes. This helps prevent neck strain from looking down all day.

* **Arm's Length**: Sit about an arm's length away from your screen. You should be able to read text comfortably without leaning in.

* **Reduce Glare**: Position your desk so a window isn't directly behind or in front of your screen. If you have glare, try rotating your setup or closing blinds. Adjust your screen brightness and contrast.

**Lighting: See Clearly, Reduce Strain**

Good lighting makes a big difference in how tired your eyes feel.

* **Natural Light**: If possible, position your desk near a window, but not directly facing it or with your back to it (which causes screen glare).

* **Task Lamp**: A simple, adjustable desk lamp is great for focused light. Point the light at your work surface, not directly at your screen or into your eyes.

* **Warm Light**: Choose a light bulb with a "warm" color temperature (around 2700K-3000K). This is generally softer on the eyes than harsh blue or bright white light.

**Take Regular Breaks: Your Best Ergonomic Tool**

Even a perfectly set up workspace can't beat movement.

* **The 20-20-20 Rule**: Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for at least 20 seconds. This helps your eyes relax and refocus.

* **Stretch and Move**: Every hour, stand up, stretch your arms, legs, and back, and walk around for a few minutes. Get some water. This prevents stiffness and improves circulation throughout your body.
