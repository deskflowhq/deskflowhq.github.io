Budget Ergonomics for Your Desk

Published 2026-08-30

You don't need expensive gear to make your home office more comfortable and prevent strain. Many effective ergonomic improvements can be made with items you already own or for very little cost. This guide focuses on simple adjustments for a better posture.

Your Chair: The Foundation

Start with the chair you have, whether it's a dining chair or a basic office chair. The goal is to support your back and get your body in a good position relative to your desk.

* **Seat Height:** If your chair is too low, use a firm cushion, a folded towel, or a small pillow to raise yourself. Your hips should be slightly higher than your knees.

* **Back Support:** Roll up a small towel or use a thin pillow and place it behind the curve of your lower back (your lumbar spine). This supports the natural curve of your back and helps you sit upright.

* **Sit Back:** Always try to sit all the way back in your chair so your back is against the support. Don't perch on the edge.

Screen Height: Eye Level is Key

Your computer screen should be positioned so the top of the screen is at or slightly below your eye level. This prevents neck strain from looking down or craning your neck up.

* **Raise Your Monitor:** Stack sturdy books, reams of paper, or strong cardboard boxes under your monitor to bring it to the correct height. Make sure it's stable and won't tip over.

* **Laptop Users:** If you use a laptop, raise it on books or a sturdy stand. This will likely make typing awkward, so pair this with an external keyboard and mouse (see next section).

* **Distance:** Position the screen about an arm's length away from you.

Keyboard and Mouse: Hand and Wrist Comfort

Proper positioning for your keyboard and mouse prevents wrist and shoulder pain.

* **Flat Wrists:** When typing or mousing, your wrists should be straight and neutral, not bent up, down, or to the side. Your hands should float over the keyboard, not rest on the desk.

* **Forearm Support:** Your forearms should be roughly parallel to the floor. If your desk is too high, raise your chair (and use a footrest).

* **Close to Body:** Keep your keyboard and mouse close to your body to avoid reaching, which can strain your shoulders.

Foot Support: Ground Yourself

Your feet should be flat on the floor or on a stable footrest. This helps distribute your weight and reduces pressure on your legs and lower back.

* **DIY Footrest:** If your feet don't comfortably reach the floor, use a stack of sturdy books, a strong shoebox, a small stool, or even a ream of paper as a footrest. Ensure it's stable and large enough for both feet.

* **No Dangling:** Avoid having your feet dangle unsupported.

Breaks: Your Best Ergonomic Tool

Even the most perfectly set up workstation isn't healthy if you sit in it for hours without moving. Movement is crucial.

* **Regular Movement:** Every 30-60 minutes, stand up, stretch, walk around, or grab a drink of water. This improves circulation and reduces stiffness.

* **Eye Breaks:** Follow the 20-20-20 rule: Every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds. This helps reduce eye strain.

Small Changes, Big Impact

Improving your ergonomic setup doesn't require a big budget. By using household items and being mindful of your posture, you can create a much more comfortable and productive workspace. Listen to your body and adjust as needed – small changes can make a huge difference in how you feel at the end of the day.
