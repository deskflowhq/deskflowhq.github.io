Ergonomic Basics for Cheap

Published 2026-09-15

Working from home often means using whatever surface you have – a kitchen table, a small desk, or even your couch. But long hours in a bad posture can lead to aches and pains. This guide will help you improve your comfort and posture without buying expensive ergonomic chairs or fancy equipment. We'll focus on small, free, or very low-cost changes using things you likely already own.

**Your Chair (The Most Important Part)**

Use what you have, and make it better. Most people don't have an adjustable office chair to start. A dining chair or basic desk chair is common.

**Back Support:** If your chair doesn't have good lumbar support (the curve for your lower back), roll up a small towel or a cushion and place it in the small of your back. Experiment with its position until it feels supportive.

**Foot Support:** Are your feet flat on the floor? If not, they should be. If your chair is too high, use a stack of old books, a sturdy shoebox, or even a small wastebasket turned upside down as a footrest. This helps stabilize your posture.

**Seat Comfort:** If your chair seat is hard, add a pillow or a folded blanket for extra cushioning. This can prevent numbness and discomfort over long periods.

**Screen Height Matters**

Your screen should be at eye level. When you look straight ahead, your eyes should meet the top third of your screen. This prevents neck strain from looking down.

**Laptop Users:** This is crucial. Laptops are terrible for ergonomics because the screen is attached to the keyboard. To raise your laptop screen, stack sturdy books, old phone books, or even a robust box underneath it. Make sure it's stable.

**External Monitor Users:** If you have a separate monitor, use books or a ream of printer paper to raise it to eye level.

**Keyboard and Mouse Positioning**

Your keyboard and mouse should be close enough that your arms form roughly a 90-degree angle at the elbow, and your shoulders are relaxed, not hunched.

**Separate Keyboard/Mouse for Laptops:** If you raised your laptop screen (as in the previous tip), you *must* use an external keyboard and mouse. You can find basic wired sets very cheaply (often under $20) online or at electronics stores.

**Wrist Position:** Try to keep your wrists straight, not bent up or down. Avoid resting your wrists on the desk while typing. A small rolled towel can act as a DIY wrist rest if needed, but the best approach is to "float" your wrists above the keyboard.

**Light and Glare**

Position your screen so that light sources (windows, overhead lights) are not directly behind it or reflecting off it. Reflections cause glare and eye strain.

**Natural Light is Best:** If possible, sit facing a window or with a window to your side. Avoid having a bright window directly behind you, as it creates a silhouette and makes your screen harder to see.

**Adjust Brightness:** Make sure your screen brightness matches the room's brightness. Too bright or too dim can strain your eyes.

**Movement is Key**

No matter how good your setup, staying in one position for hours is bad. Every 30-60 minutes, stand up, stretch, and walk around for a few minutes. Get a glass of water or do a quick chore.

Even small shifts in posture can help. Lean back, lean forward, shift your weight. Your body is designed to move.

**DIY Solutions and Scavenging**

Before buying anything, look for items you already own that can serve a new purpose. Old shoeboxes, sturdy books, blankets, towels, cushions, small stools, or even a sturdy stack of magazines can be repurposed.

If you absolutely need a cheap external keyboard/mouse, check thrift stores, online marketplaces (like Facebook Marketplace or Craigslist), or ask friends if they have spares. You might be surprised what you can find for free or very little money.

You don't need to spend a fortune to be comfortable and pain-free while working from home. By making simple adjustments with items you already own, you can create a much more ergonomic and healthier workspace. Listen to your body, make small changes, and move frequently. Your back and neck will thank you!
