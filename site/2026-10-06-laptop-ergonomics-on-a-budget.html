Laptop Ergonomics on a Budget

Published 2026-10-06

**Introduction**

Working on a laptop can quickly lead to neck, shoulder, and back pain. This happens because your screen is low, forcing you to constantly look down. The good news is you can fix this common problem for very little money. This guide will show you how to improve your posture and comfort using just a few cheap items.

**The Problem: Laptop Hunch**

When you use a laptop directly on a table, your screen is too low, and your keyboard is too close to the screen. This makes you hunch forward and crane your neck. Over time, this causes discomfort and can lead to more serious issues.

**The Simple Solution: The Magic Trio**

To fix laptop hunch, you need three key items. These will help you elevate your screen and separate your input devices (keyboard and mouse).

1. **Laptop Stand** (or a DIY alternative)

2. **External Keyboard**

3. **External Mouse**

Let's look at each one.

**1. Elevate Your Screen: Laptop Stand**

Your monitor should be at eye level. When your laptop is on a stand, the top of its screen should be roughly level with your eyes. This keeps your neck straight.

* **Why it's important:** Prevents neck strain and forward head posture.

* **Cheap options:**

* **DIY:** Stack a few sturdy books, a shoebox, or even reams of paper. Make sure it's stable and won't wobble.

* **Budget Stands:** Simple plastic or metal laptop stands can be found for $15-$25 online. Look for basic, non-adjustable models.

* **Tip:** If your DIY stand makes your laptop slide, place a thin non-slip mat (like shelf liner) underneath it.

**2. Use an External Keyboard**

Once your laptop is elevated, its built-in keyboard will be too high to use comfortably. You need a separate keyboard that sits on your desk.

* **Why it's important:** Allows you to type with your elbows at a comfortable 90-degree angle, close to your body. Your wrists should be flat, not bent up or down.

* **Cheap options:**

* **Basic Wired Keyboard:** A simple USB keyboard can cost as little as $10-$20 new. Brands like Amazon Basics or Logitech offer good entry-level models.

* **Used:** Check thrift stores or online marketplaces for used wired keyboards. They often cost less than $5.

* **Tip:** Wired keyboards are generally cheaper and don't need batteries. They work with almost any computer.

**3. Get an External Mouse**

Just like the keyboard, a separate mouse allows for a more natural arm and shoulder position.

* **Why it's important:** Prevents reaching and awkward wrist angles. Reduces strain on your shoulder and arm.

* **Cheap options:**

* **Basic Wired Mouse:** A simple optical USB mouse can be found for $7-$15 new. Again, Amazon Basics or Logitech are good starting points.

* **Used:** Thrift stores often have wired mice for a few dollars.

* **Tip:** Wired optical mice are very reliable and don't require mouse pads on most surfaces.

**Setting Up Your New Workspace**

Now that you have your trio, here’s how to arrange it:

1. Place your laptop on your stand (or stack of books).

2. Position the external keyboard directly in front of you, on the desk.

3. Place the external mouse next to your keyboard, on the side you prefer.

4. Sit with your back straight, feet flat on the floor (or a footrest).

5. Adjust the stand so the top of your laptop screen is at or slightly below eye level.

6. Ensure your elbows form roughly a 90-degree angle when typing, and your wrists are straight.

**Total Cost Estimate**

For brand new basic items, you can expect to spend around $30-$50 for a stand, keyboard, and mouse. If you use DIY options and look for used items, you might spend less than $10-$20. It's a small investment for a big improvement in comfort and productivity.

**Where to Buy**

* **New:** Amazon, Walmart, Target, Best Buy, office supply stores. Look for their "basic" or "essential" lines.

* **Used/DIY:** Thrift stores (Goodwill, Salvation Army), Facebook Marketplace, local classifieds, your own bookshelves or closets.

**Don't Forget to Take Breaks!**

Even with a perfectly ergonomic setup, sitting for long periods isn't healthy. Remember to stand up, stretch, and walk around for 5-10 minutes every hour. Your body will thank you.
