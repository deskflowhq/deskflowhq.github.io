Budget Ergonomics for Your Home Office

Published 2026-08-26

Starting remote work often means using what you already have at home. But sitting uncomfortably for hours can quickly lead to pain in your back, neck, or wrists. You don't need to spend a lot of money on expensive office furniture to feel better. This guide shows you how to improve your workspace using cheap or free items you likely already own.

**Your Chair: Make it Better**

Most home chairs aren't designed for long work sessions.

* **Seat Cushion:** If your chair seat is hard, grab a firm couch cushion, a pillow, or a folded blanket. Place it on your chair for extra padding.

* **Lumbar Support:** Your lower back needs support. Roll up a towel or a small blanket tightly and place it horizontally just above your waistline, against the chair back. This helps fill the natural curve of your spine.

* **Armrests:** If your chair has hard armrests, wrap them in a scarf or small towel for softer support. Your arms should be roughly at desk height when typing, not shrugging your shoulders.

**Screen Height: Lift it Up**

Your monitor or laptop screen should be at eye level. This prevents you from craning your neck.

* **Laptop Users:** Stack sturdy books, reams of paper, or a stable cardboard box under your laptop. The top of your screen should be level with your eyes when you sit upright.

* **External Monitor Users:** Do the same. Ensure whatever you use is stable and won't wobble.

* **Important Note:** If you raise your laptop, you will need a separate keyboard and mouse (see next section).

**Keyboard and Mouse: Separate is Key**

Using your laptop's built-in keyboard when it's raised can hurt your wrists and shoulders.

* **External Setup:** Invest in a basic external keyboard and mouse. You can find simple wired sets for under $20-30 new, or even cheaper secondhand. This allows you to keep your hands and wrists in a neutral position, close to your body.

* **Positioning:** Keep your keyboard close to you so your elbows are bent at about a 90-degree angle. Your mouse should be right beside it.

**Foot Support: Ground Your Feet**

Your feet should be flat on the floor or a footrest. This helps posture and circulation.

* **DIY Footrest:** If your feet dangle or don't feel fully supported, use a sturdy shoebox, a stack of large books, or even a small, low stool. It doesn't need to be fancy, just stable enough for both feet to rest comfortably.

**Take Breaks and Move**

This is the most powerful and cheapest ergonomic tool.

* **Regular Movement:** Set a timer to stand up and stretch every 30-60 minutes. Walk around for a minute, get a drink, or just stretch your arms and legs.

* **Micro-Breaks:** Look away from your screen every 20 minutes for 20 seconds at something 20 feet away (the 20-20-20 rule) to rest your eyes.

**Lighting: Reduce Eye Strain**

Good lighting reduces eye fatigue, which is part of overall comfort.

* **Natural Light:** Position your desk near a window if possible, but avoid direct glare on your screen.

* **Cheap Lamp:** If natural light isn't enough, use a simple desk lamp. Position it so it illuminates your workspace without causing glare on your screen. An inexpensive LED bulb can make a big difference.

**Final Thought**

You don't need a fancy setup to work comfortably and productively. Small, thoughtful adjustments with items you already own can make a huge difference in preventing pain and improving your focus. Listen to your body and adjust your setup as needed.
