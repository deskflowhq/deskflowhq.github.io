Ergonomic Workspace from What You Have

Published 2026-10-15

Getting started with remote work often means using whatever desk, chair, and equipment you already own. You don't need expensive furniture to make your workspace more comfortable and healthy. This guide shows you how to improve your setup's ergonomics using household items or very cheap additions.

**Your Chair Matters**

Even a basic kitchen chair can be made better. Sit all the way back so your hips touch the backrest. Use a rolled towel, a small pillow, or even a cushion from the couch behind your lower back for lumbar support. This helps maintain the natural curve of your spine. Adjust your sitting position so your feet are flat on the floor.

**Screen at Eye Level**

The top of your screen should be roughly at eye level. This prevents neck strain from looking down all day. For a monitor, stack sturdy books, reams of paper, or a shoebox under it until it's high enough. If you're using a laptop, do the same. This will make your laptop's keyboard too high, which leads to the next step.

**Keyboard and Mouse Comfort**

If you've raised your laptop screen, you absolutely need a separate keyboard and mouse. You can find very basic, wired sets for under $20 at many electronics or even discount stores. Place the keyboard close to you so your wrists stay straight and flat, not bent up or down. Your mouse should also be close, allowing your arm to stay relaxed and close to your body. Avoid reaching far for it.

**Foot Support**

Your feet should be flat on the floor or a sturdy footrest. If your chair is too high and you can't adjust it, use a stack of books, a sturdy cardboard box, or a small stool as a footrest. This helps maintain good posture, improves circulation, and takes pressure off your lower back and legs.

**Lighting it Right**

Position your workspace so light sources, like windows or bright lamps, are not directly behind your screen or directly behind you, causing glare. Ideally, light should come from the side, or you should have a small lamp illuminating your immediate work area. Soft, diffused light is generally best to reduce eye strain.

**Take Breaks Often**

No matter how perfectly ergonomic your setup is, sitting for long periods is unhealthy. Set a timer to remind you to stand up, stretch, and walk around for 5-10 minutes every hour. Hydrate regularly and give your eyes a break by looking away from your screen at a distant object for 20 seconds every 20 minutes.
