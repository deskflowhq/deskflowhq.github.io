Basic Ergonomics for Any Budget Desk

Published 2026-04-24

Welcome to working from home! You don't need fancy gear to be comfortable and productive. This guide focuses on simple, cheap ways to improve your posture and reduce strain, even if you're using a kitchen table or a borrowed chair.

### Why Ergonomics Matters

Ergonomics simply means designing your workspace to fit you. Doing it right prevents aches, pains, and fatigue. This means you can work longer and feel better at the end of the day.

### Your Chair: The Foundation

Even if your chair isn't an "office chair," you can improve it.

* **Back Support:** Use a small pillow or a rolled-up towel at your lower back's curve. This helps maintain your spine's natural S-shape.

* **Feet Flat:** Your feet should be flat on the floor or a footrest. If they dangle, use a stack of books, a sturdy shoebox, or even a ream of paper as a makeshift footrest.

* **Seat Height:** Aim for your hips to be slightly higher than your knees. If your chair is too low, add a firm cushion. If it's too high and you can't adjust it, use that footrest strategy.

### Screen Height: Eye Level is Key

Your monitor or laptop screen should be at arm's length, with the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level.

* **Laptop Users:** This is crucial. Laptops force you to look down, straining your neck. Elevate your laptop! Use a stack of books, sturdy boxes, or even a cheap laptop stand.

* **External Monitor:** Adjust the stand if it has one. If not, use books or boxes under it until it's at the right height.

* **External Keyboard/Mouse:** If you elevate your laptop, you absolutely need an external keyboard and mouse so your hands aren't reaching up. A basic wired set is very cheap.

### Keyboard and Mouse: Hand and Wrist Health

Once your screen is elevated (especially for laptops), you need separate input devices.

* **Flat Wrists:** Keep your wrists straight and flat while typing and mousing. Don't bend them up or down.

* **Close to Body:** Keep your keyboard and mouse close to your body to avoid reaching, which strains your shoulders.

* **Cheap Options:** Basic wired keyboards and mice can be found for under $20 total at most electronics stores or online. Look for comfortable shapes.

### Lighting: Avoid Strain

Good lighting reduces eye strain and headaches.

* **Natural Light:** Position your desk so natural light comes from the side, not directly in front or behind your screen. Direct light can cause glare.

* **Desk Lamp:** A simple, adjustable desk lamp can make a huge difference, especially in the evenings. Direct light onto your workspace, not your screen. Avoid harsh overhead lighting that creates shadows.

### Breaks: The Best Ergonomic Tool

No setup is perfect without movement.

* **Short, Frequent Breaks:** Every 30-60 minutes, stand up, stretch, walk around for a few minutes.

* **Look Away:** Give your eyes a break by looking at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds every 20 minutes (the 20-20-20 rule).

* **Stretch:** Gently stretch your neck, shoulders, wrists, and back. There are many free "desk stretch" videos online.

### Start Small, Feel Better

You don't need to buy everything at once. Pick one or two areas that cause you the most discomfort and start there. Even small changes can make a big difference in how you feel and your productivity.
