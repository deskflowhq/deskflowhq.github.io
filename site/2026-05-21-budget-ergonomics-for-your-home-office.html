Budget Ergonomics for Your Home Office

Published 2026-05-21

Working from home can quickly lead to aches and pains if your setup isn't comfortable. You don't need expensive equipment to make your workspace more ergonomic. This guide focuses on simple, cheap adjustments to improve your comfort and health using items you likely already have.

### Your Chair: The Foundation

Your chair is where you spend most of your time. Even a basic kitchen chair can be improved.

**Back Support:**

Roll up a towel or use a small pillow. Place it in the curve of your lower back (lumbar region). This helps maintain the natural S-shape of your spine and prevents slouching.

**Seat Comfort:**

If your chair is hard, use a folded blanket, a spare cushion, or even a firm pillow to pad the seat. This reduces pressure on your hips and tailbone.

**Proper Height:**

Your feet should be flat on the floor or a footrest, and your elbows should be at roughly a 90-degree angle when typing. If your chair is too low, add more seat padding. If it's too high and not adjustable, focus on foot support.

### Monitor Height: Eye-Level Comfort

Looking down at a screen for hours strains your neck and shoulders. Your monitor's top edge should be at or slightly below eye level.

**Use What You Have:**

Stack sturdy books, reams of paper, or a sturdy cardboard box under your monitor or laptop. Ensure it's stable and won't wobble. This brings the screen up to eye level, keeping your neck straight.

**Laptop Users:**

If you use a laptop, elevating it means you'll need an external keyboard and mouse. These can be found cheaply online or at thrift stores.

### Keyboard and Mouse: Hand & Wrist Health

Proper positioning prevents wrist strain and discomfort.

**Keep Wrists Straight:**

When typing, your wrists should be straight, not bent up or down. Your keyboard should be close enough that your elbows are at your sides.

**DIY Wrist Rest:**

Fold a small towel or use a soft, narrow object like a scarf to create a makeshift wrist rest for your keyboard and mouse. It should support your palm, not your wrist, to avoid pressure on nerves.

**Mouse Position:**

Keep your mouse close to your keyboard to avoid reaching too far, which can strain your shoulder.

### Foot Support: Grounding Yourself

Having your feet properly supported can improve posture and circulation.

**Find a Footrest:**

If your feet don't touch the floor comfortably when sitting ergonomically, use a sturdy box, a stack of books, or even an old phone book as a footrest. Your knees should be at about a 90-degree angle, or slightly higher than your hips.

### Lighting: Reduce Strain

Good lighting reduces eye strain and can improve your mood.

**Natural Light First:**

Position your desk near a window if possible, but avoid direct glare on your screen. If light from a window is behind you, it can cause glare on your monitor.

**DIY Task Lighting:**

If natural light isn't enough, use a spare table lamp. Position it so it illuminates your workspace without casting shadows on your screen or shining directly into your eyes. Avoid working in a dark room with only screen light.

### Take Breaks: The Cheapest Ergonomic Tool

No matter how perfect your setup, sitting for too long is bad for you.

**Move Regularly:**

Set a timer to remind yourself to stand up, stretch, and walk around for 5-10 minutes every hour. This is free, incredibly effective, and prevents stiffness and fatigue.

**Simple Stretches:**

Roll your shoulders, gently stretch your neck from side to side, and reach your arms overhead. These small movements make a big difference.

By implementing these simple, cheap adjustments, you can create a much more comfortable and supportive home office environment without breaking the bank. Your body will thank you!
