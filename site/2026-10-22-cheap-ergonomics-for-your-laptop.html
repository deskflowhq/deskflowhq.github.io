Cheap Ergonomics for Your Laptop

Published 2026-10-22

Working from home with just a laptop often leads to neck, back, and wrist pain. You don't need to spend a lot to make your workspace comfortable. With a few household items and one smart, cheap purchase, you can create a much better setup. The goal is to get your screen to eye level and use a separate keyboard and mouse.

Your Chair is Key

Start with your chair. Any sturdy chair can work – a dining chair, kitchen stool, or even a basic office chair. The most important thing is that your feet can be flat on the floor, or supported by something like a shoebox or stack of books. If your chair is too low, add a firm pillow or folded towel to sit on. If your back isn't supported, roll up a towel or small blanket and place it in the curve of your lower back.

Raise Your Screen (Not Your Neck!)

This is the biggest game-changer for neck pain. Your laptop screen should be at eye level. When you look straight ahead, your eyes should meet the top third of your screen. To do this, stack sturdy books, shoeboxes, or even old textbooks under your laptop until it's high enough. Make sure the stack is stable and won't wobble.

External Keyboard and Mouse (The One Cheap Essential)

Once your laptop is elevated, you can't type on its keyboard comfortably. For proper ergonomics, you absolutely need an external keyboard and mouse. This is usually the only item you might need to buy. You can find basic wired sets for $15-$25 online or at any electronics store. This investment lets you keep your wrists straight and arms relaxed while typing, preventing strain. If you can borrow a set from a friend or your company, that's even better!

Arm Support for Comfort

When typing, your forearms should be roughly parallel to the floor. If your desk is too high, you might need to raise your chair (and use a footrest). If your arms hang, you can use a pillow or a rolled-up towel to support your wrists or forearms *gently* while typing. Don't rest your wrists directly on the desk for long periods; your hands should float slightly above the keyboard.

Take Breaks Often

No matter how perfect your setup, sitting still for hours is bad for your body. Set a timer for every 30-60 minutes. Stand up, stretch, walk around, grab water. Even a minute or two makes a huge difference in preventing stiffness and improving focus.

Listen to Your Body

Pay attention to any discomfort. If your neck hurts, maybe your screen isn't high enough. If your wrists ache, check your keyboard and mouse position. Adjust things until you feel comfortable. Small changes can prevent big pains and keep you productive.
