Lift Your Laptop, Save Your Neck

Published 2026-04-19

When you start working remotely, it’s common to just use your laptop on a table. But looking down at your screen for hours and using a cramped keyboard can cause neck pain, shoulder aches, and wrist discomfort. This guide shows you how to fix that for cheap.

**The Problem: Laptop Neck and Wrist**

Your laptop screen forces your head to tilt forward, straining your neck. Its built-in keyboard and trackpad also put your wrists and hands in awkward positions, especially if you use them for a long time. These small issues add up to big discomfort and even injury over weeks and months.

**The Simple Solution: Get Separate Gear**

The key is to separate your screen from your keyboard and mouse. You need a way to lift your laptop screen to eye level and then use external accessories for typing and clicking. This allows your neck to stay straight and your wrists to stay neutral.

**What You Need (and Why)**

You only need three items to dramatically improve your setup:

* **A Laptop Stand:** To raise your screen.

* **An External Keyboard:** To type with your wrists in a natural position.

* **An External Mouse:** To control your computer without awkward trackpad gestures.

**Finding a Laptop Stand**

Your goal is to get the top of your laptop screen roughly level with your eyes when you sit upright.

* **Free Option:** Start by stacking sturdy books or a stable box underneath your laptop. Make sure it's completely stable and won't wobble.

* **Budget Option (under $30):** Look for basic plastic or metal laptop stands online or at electronics stores. Many fixed-height stands are very affordable. Some simple adjustable ones might also be in this price range. You don’t need anything fancy; just something that lifts the laptop reliably.

**Choosing an External Keyboard**

Using a separate keyboard lets you keep your hands and wrists relaxed.

* **Budget Option (under $20):** A wired USB keyboard is the cheapest and most reliable option. You can find basic membrane keyboards from brands like Logitech, Dell, or Amazon Basics for $10-20. They plug directly into your laptop's USB port. No need for wireless, batteries, or mechanical switches when you're starting out.

**Picking an External Mouse**

A mouse is far more comfortable and efficient than a laptop trackpad for extended use.

* **Budget Option (under $15):** Similar to the keyboard, a wired USB optical mouse is your best bet. Basic models are inexpensive ($8-15) and plug into another USB port. They are precise, reliable, and comfortable enough for daily use.

**Setting It All Up**

1. **Position Your Laptop:** Place your laptop on its stand (or books) directly in front of you. Adjust the height so the top third of the screen is at or slightly below your eye level.

2. **Connect Keyboard and Mouse:** Plug your external keyboard and mouse into your laptop's USB ports. If you don't have enough ports, a cheap USB hub ($10-15) can expand them.

3. **Arrange Your Workspace:** Place the external keyboard directly in front of you, close enough that your elbows stay at your sides. Your mouse should be right next to the keyboard, easily reachable without stretching.

**Budget Breakdown Example**

You can achieve this comfortable setup for less than the cost of a few take-out meals:

* **Laptop Stand:** $25 (for a decent basic model)

* **Wired Keyboard:** $15

* **Wired Mouse:** $10

* **Total Estimated Cost: $50**

This small investment will make a huge difference in your comfort and ability to focus while working from home.

**Beyond the Basics**

Once you have this basic setup, you can always upgrade components later if you find specific needs (e.g., a wireless keyboard/mouse, a monitor, a better chair). But for a beginner on a tight budget, lifting your laptop screen and using external input devices is the absolute best first step to a healthier home office.
