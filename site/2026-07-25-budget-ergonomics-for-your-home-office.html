Budget Ergonomics for Your Home Office

Published 2026-07-25

Setting up a home office doesn't have to be expensive. You can make your workspace much more comfortable and prevent strain without buying fancy gear. This guide focuses on simple, cheap ways to improve your ergonomic setup.

### Raise Your Screen to Eye Level

Looking down at a laptop for hours is a common cause of neck and shoulder pain. Get your screen higher.

**The Fix:** Prop up your laptop or monitor.

**How to Do It Cheaply:**

* **Books:** A stable stack of old textbooks or hardcover books works perfectly. Make sure they are sturdy.

* **Sturdy Box:** A shoebox, a small delivery box, or a wooden crate can also work.

* **DIY Stand:** If you have some scrap wood or sturdy cardboard, you can build a simple riser.

**Goal:** The top of your screen should be roughly at your eye level. This helps you keep your head in a neutral position.

### Use an External Keyboard and Mouse

Once your laptop is propped up, using its built-in keyboard and trackpad becomes awkward. This forces your wrists into bad positions.

**The Fix:** Connect separate input devices.

**How to Do It Cheaply:**

* **Basic USB Set:** You can find a basic wired USB keyboard and mouse combo for under $20 at most electronics stores or online.

* **Thrift Stores:** Check local second-hand shops for used keyboards and mice. Clean them thoroughly before use.

* **Ask Friends:** Someone you know might have an old spare keyboard and mouse they no longer need.

**Goal:** Your wrists should be straight, not bent up or down. Your forearms should be parallel to the floor, with your elbows bent at roughly a 90-degree angle.

### Improve Your Chair Comfort

You don't need an expensive ergonomic chair to start. You can make a basic chair much better.

**The Fix:** Add support where your body needs it.

**How to Do It Cheaply:**

* **Lumbar Support:** Roll up a small towel, a T-shirt, or a thin blanket and place it in the small of your back (the curve just above your hips).

* **Seat Cushion:** A firm pillow, an old couch cushion, or a folded blanket can add padding to a hard seat. Check discount stores for cheap cushions.

* **Adjust Height:** If your chair height is adjustable, set it so your feet are flat on the floor (or a footrest) and your knees are at a 90-degree angle.

**Goal:** Your back should be supported, especially in the lower curve of your spine. Your hips should be slightly higher than your knees if possible.

### Don't Forget Your Feet

Having your feet dangle without support can put stress on your lower back and legs.

**The Fix:** Give your feet a place to rest firmly.

**How to Do It Cheaply:**

* **Shoebox/Crate:** A sturdy shoebox, a small wooden crate, or a plastic storage bin turned upside down can serve as a footrest.

* **Stack of Books/Magazines:** Use a stable stack of heavy books or old magazines.

* **Small Stool:** If you have a small, sturdy stool lying around, use it.

**Goal:** Your feet should be flat on the footrest. Your knees should be at a 90-degree angle or slightly more open.

### Take Regular Breaks and Move

Even with a perfect setup, sitting still for too long is unhealthy. Movement is a key part of good ergonomics, and it's free.

**The Fix:** Incorporate short, frequent breaks.

**How to Do It For Free:**

* **The 20-20-20 Rule:** Every 20 minutes, look away from your screen at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds. This rests your eyes.

* **Stand Up and Stretch:** Every 30-60 minutes, stand up, walk around for a few minutes, stretch your arms, legs, and back.

* **Hydrate Often:** Keep a water bottle nearby and get up to refill it frequently.

**Goal:** Break up long periods of sitting to improve circulation, reduce stiffness, and prevent mental fatigue.

These simple, low-cost adjustments can make a significant difference in your comfort and long-term health while working from home. Start with one change and build from there.
