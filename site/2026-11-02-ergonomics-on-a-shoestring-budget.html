Ergonomics on a Shoestring Budget

Published 2026-11-02

Starting remote work often means using the furniture you already have. Don't worry if you don't have a fancy ergonomic chair or an adjustable desk. With a few simple tricks and common household items, you can make your current setup much more comfortable and prevent strain. This guide focuses on using what you have or buying items for under $20-30.

**Why Basic Ergonomics Matters**

Working long hours in an uncomfortable position can lead to neck pain, backaches, wrist strain, and fatigue. Spending a little time optimizing your space now can prevent these issues, improve your focus, and make your workday much more pleasant.

**Your Chair: The Foundation of Comfort**

* **Back Support:** Most dining chairs or basic office chairs lack good lower back (lumbar) support. Roll up a hand towel or use a small cushion/pillow. Place it horizontally in the curve of your lower back, just above your waistline. This helps maintain your spine's natural curve.

* **Footrest:** Your feet should be flat on the floor or a footrest. If your chair is too high, or your feet don't touch comfortably, use a sturdy stack of old books, a small cardboard box, or even a ream of paper as a makeshift footrest. This relieves pressure from your legs and lower back.

* **Seat Comfort:** If your chair's seat is too hard, a simple cushion, a folded blanket, or a thick towel can add much-needed padding for long sitting periods.

**Your Desk/Table: Getting the Right Height**

* **Elbow Angle:** When you sit upright, your forearms should be roughly parallel to the floor, forming about a 90-100 degree angle at your elbows when typing. If your table is too low, you might slouch. If too high, your shoulders will hunch.

* **Laptop Users: This is Critical!** A laptop screen is rarely at the right height when its keyboard is usable. You'll often find yourself looking down, straining your neck.

* **Raise Your Laptop Screen:** Stack sturdy books, use a robust cardboard box, or even a shoebox filled with heavy items to raise your laptop. The goal is for the top of your screen to be at or slightly below eye level.

* **Add an External Keyboard and Mouse:** This is the *single most important* cheap upgrade for laptop users. You can find basic USB keyboards and mice for $10-20 total online or at big box stores. This allows you to raise your laptop screen independently while keeping your hands at a comfortable, low height on your external keyboard and mouse.

**Monitor Placement**

If you use an external monitor (instead of or in addition to a laptop), adjust its height so the top of the screen is at or slightly below eye level. Again, use books or a sturdy box underneath if needed.

**Keyboard and Mouse: Simple Comfort**

Even for desktop users, ensure your mouse is close to your keyboard to avoid reaching, which can strain your shoulder. A cheap mouse pad can also improve comfort and tracking.

**Lighting: Reduce Eye Strain**

Position your screen to avoid glare from windows or bright overhead lights. If working in a dim area, use a desk lamp to provide soft, even lighting on your workspace. A basic LED desk lamp can often be found for under $15.

**Don't Forget Breaks!**

No ergonomic setup is perfect if you sit for hours without moving. This is free and essential.

* Set a timer for every 30-60 minutes.

* Stand up, stretch, walk around for a few minutes. Grab water, look out a window (to shift your eye focus), or do a quick chore. Your body will thank you.

**Your Budget-Friendly Ergonomic Checklist:**

* **Back Support:** Rolled towel or small pillow. (Free/Existing)

* **Footrest:** Books or sturdy box. (Free/Existing)

* **Laptop/Monitor Stand:** Books or sturdy box. (Free/Existing)

* **External Keyboard & Mouse:** ~$10-20 for basic models. (Cheap buy)

* **Seat Cushion (Optional):** Folded blanket or cheap cushion. (Free/Existing or cheap buy)

* **Desk Lamp (Optional):** ~$15-20 for a basic LED. (Cheap buy)
