Your First Workspace Without a Desk

Published 2026-10-19

Many people starting remote work or a home business don't have space or money for a dedicated desk. You can still create a functional and even comfortable setup using items you already own. This guide helps you get started cheap, turning existing furniture into your temporary office.

**Find Your Workspace Corner**

Look around your home. Where is there a flat surface that isn't always in use?

Consider a dining table, kitchen counter, a sturdy console table, or even a dresser.

Think about lighting. A spot near a window is great for natural light and mood.

Choose a place where you can minimize distractions if possible, even if it's just for a few hours.

**Improvise Your "Desk" Surface**

Your dining table or kitchen counter is often the easiest starting point. It's usually sturdy and at a good height for sitting or standing (if it's a higher counter).

If you need a smaller, more portable surface, a TV tray, a sturdy storage bin with a flat lid, or even a laundry basket turned upside down can work in a pinch for a laptop.

For an "L-shape" setup, use a small side table or stool next to your main surface to hold a notepad or coffee cup.

**Get Comfortable Seating**

You don't need an expensive office chair. Start with a dining chair.

Add a cushion for your seat if it's too hard. A regular couch cushion or a firm pillow can work.

For back support, roll up a towel or small blanket and place it in the curve of your lower back.

If you're using a couch or armchair, try to sit up straight and avoid slouching. Use pillows to prop yourself up if needed.

**Prioritize Basic Ergonomics**

This is about comfort and avoiding pain, even with a makeshift setup.

**Screen Height:** Your computer screen should be roughly arm's length away, with the top of the screen at or slightly below eye level. Use books, sturdy boxes, or even extra board games to prop up your laptop or monitor.

**Keyboard and Mouse:** If using a propped-up laptop, try to get a cheap external keyboard and mouse. This allows you to keep your hands at a comfortable level while your screen is higher. You can find basic sets for under $30.

**Feet Flat:** Your feet should ideally be flat on the floor. If they dangle, use a shoebox, footstool, or a pile of sturdy books as a footrest.

**Take Breaks:** No matter your setup, stand up and stretch every hour. This is the cheapest and most effective ergonomic tip.

**Keep it Tidy and Flexible**

Since your workspace isn't permanent, easy setup and teardown are key.

Use a small bin or tote to store your work essentials (laptop charger, notebook, pens) when you're done for the day. This keeps your home from feeling like a permanent office.

Keep only what you absolutely need for your work session on your improvised desk. Clutter creates mental clutter.

**Your Toolkit: What You Actually Need**

1. **Your Laptop/Computer:** The obvious essential.

2. **Power Cord:** Don't forget it.

3. **Earbuds/Headphones:** Crucial for calls and focus, especially in a shared space. Your phone's earbuds are fine.

4. **Optional, but highly recommended:** An external keyboard and mouse (basic models are cheap).

5. **A Water Bottle:** Stay hydrated.
