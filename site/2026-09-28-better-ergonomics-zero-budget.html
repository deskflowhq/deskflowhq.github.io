Better Ergonomics, Zero Budget

Published 2026-09-28

Starting remote work often means using whatever furniture you have. While convenient, this can quickly lead to neck pain, backaches, and wrist strain. You don't need expensive ergonomic furniture to feel better. This guide focuses on making your existing setup more comfortable and supportive using things you already own or very cheap additions.

### Raise Your Screen

**Problem:** Most laptops sit too low on a desk, forcing you to crane your neck down to see the screen. This is a common cause of neck and shoulder pain.

**Solution:** Elevate your laptop! Stack sturdy books, an old shoebox, or even a ream of printer paper under your laptop. The goal is to bring the top of your screen to eye level. When looking straight ahead, your gaze should naturally fall on the top third of your screen.

**If you use an external monitor:** Adjust its height using its stand if possible. If not, use the same book-stacking trick.

### Get an External Keyboard and Mouse

**Problem:** Typing directly on a laptop that's been raised means your arms are held high and your wrists are bent at an awkward angle. This strains your wrists and shoulders.

**Solution:** This is often the first and most impactful cheap upgrade. An external keyboard and mouse lets you keep your screen high and your typing surface low. You can find basic wired sets new for under $20, or even cheaper second-hand.

**How to set them up:** Place the keyboard close enough so your forearms are parallel to the floor and your wrists are straight. Keep your mouse close to your body to avoid overreaching. This allows your shoulders to relax.

### Improve Your Chair Comfort

**Problem:** Dining chairs, kitchen stools, or basic office chairs often lack lumbar support and proper padding, leading to back pain and discomfort.

**Solution 1: Lumbar Support:** Roll up a towel, a small blanket, or a thin pillow. Place it horizontally in the curve of your lower back, just above your waistline. This supports the natural inward curve of your spine. Experiment with how thick it needs to be.

**Solution 2: Seat Comfort:** Add extra padding to a hard seat. Use a spare cushion from a couch, a folded blanket, or a thick pillow. This can relieve pressure points and make long sitting periods more tolerable.

**Solution 3: Foot Support:** If your feet don't comfortably touch the floor, use a shoebox, a stack of sturdy books, or a small stool as a footrest. This helps maintain good posture and reduces pressure on your thighs.

### Smart Lighting

**Problem:** Poor lighting causes eye strain, headaches, and reduces focus. Glare from windows or overhead lights can be particularly disruptive.

**Solution:** Position your workspace so you have natural light from a window to your side, rather than directly behind or in front of your screen. If natural light isn't available or sufficient, use an existing desk lamp or even a bedside lamp to illuminate your work area. Avoid working in a dark room with only the screen as a light source. The goal is even, diffused light to reduce contrast.

### Take Regular Breaks

**Problem:** Even with the best setup, sitting for too long without movement is bad for your body. Stiffness and fatigue can set in.

**Solution:** This is the cheapest and most effective ergonomic tool: get up and move! Set a timer to remind you to stand up every 30-60 minutes. Walk around for a few minutes, stretch your arms, shoulders, neck, and legs. Even a short break helps reset your body and mind, reducing the cumulative strain of sitting.
